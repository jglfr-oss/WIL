A COMIC FOR HUDSONSHINE
From Dad.
ONE
Hudson was thirteen the morning he woke up glowing.
WHAT.
He tried a hoodie. Sunglasses. Standing in the dark. It didn't matter. Gold came through everything.
I know. I've been waiting for this.
Dad had it too. Faded now. But he'd never lost it.
It's not a curse. It's power. But you're old enough to hear the rest of it straight.
TWO
The glow made him fast.
ZOOM
Strong. Steady. Hard to knock down.
But the same thing that made him strong made him seen. Everywhere he went, people looked first.
The store guy who followed him down every aisle. His friends got to browse.
The officer who slowed down and stared. Just stared. Didn't do that to anyone else on the block.
THREE
Then there was the crew. Same school, same street, same jokes since second grade.
They didn't glow. They didn't need to.
Bikes. Basketball. Xbox till one in the morning. Real friends.
Go. Have fun. I'm not telling you not to. I'm telling you to keep your eyes open.
Out there, you're the only one who glows.
When it gets dark, they can disappear. You can't.
FOUR
Friday night. The old quarry. Nobody was supposed to be there. Everybody was.
HEY! WHO'S DOWN THERE?
A flashlight. A grown-up voice. Boots on gravel.
RUN!
The crew scattered into the dark and the dark took them. Just like that. Gone.
Hudson could run faster than any of them. That wasn't the problem.
The problem was that a glowing kid running in the dark is the only thing anyone sees.
FIVE
He heard Dad's voice, clear as if he were standing there.
Speed is the small power. Control is the big one.
He stopped. Hands up. Glow steady. Voice calm.
My name is Hudson. I'm thirteen. I'm not running.
Can I call my dad? He's expecting me. He knows where I am.
Dad was there in eleven minutes. Because Dad knew exactly where to come.
Evening, officer. That's my son. Let's talk.
SIX
Saturday morning the crew showed up at the door. All four of them. Looking at their shoes.
Bro. We're sorry. We didn't even think.
They're not bad kids. They ran because they could vanish. You can't. That's the whole lesson.
You did it right. Not because you were fast. Because you were the calmest person there.

The gold protocols

  1. Your people always know where you are and who you're with. Location on, phone charged, no exceptions.
  2. If the plan changes, you text. Before it changes, not after.
  3. Never be the one left behind. No "catch up later." You leave when they leave, or you don't go.
  4. If it feels wrong, leave. You don't owe anyone a reason.
  5. If your body feels off — tired, dizzy, low — say it out loud. Don't tough it out to fit in.
  6. With adults or police: stop, hands visible, calm voice, say your name. Stay safe now. Argue later, with Dad next to you.
  7. Call home. Always. No trouble, no questions, until you're safe.
He went back out with the crew. He always went back out. He just went smart.
And he never once tried to hide the glow again.
THE END

Talk about it with Dad

1. Where in your real life do you "glow"? Where do people look at you first?

2. At the quarry, what would you have done? Be honest.

3. Which protocol would be hardest when the whole crew is doing something?

4. Ask Dad about a time he was the only one who glowed.
